2. THE PROBLEM, THE USER, WHAT IS DIFFERENT

Choosing where to plant first, with evidence

Sime Darby Property has a stated target of 50,000 endangered trees by 2030 and, in City of Elmina, a 5 km Urban Biodiversity Corridor linking six parks. Every season someone decides which sites get trees first. Heat on bare ground, gaps in the corridor and unshaded footpaths all matter, and without a repeatable method each choice is hard to explain or compare. Satellite maps do not settle it: independent maps put Elmina's tree cover anywhere from about 4% to 28%.

What makes Canopia different

PointWhat it means, with a number you can check
Measured, not assumedEvery site score is computed from satellite measurements of the township: Sentinel-2 vegetation and Landsat ground heat (cloud-masked two-year medians), plus OpenStreetMap paths, parks and buildings. The sites are simulated; the measurements under them are real.
Township-wide, tied to the corridorAll 564 candidate sites across the township are ranked, and closing gaps in the Urban Biodiversity Corridor is one of the three scores.
The best set, not just top scoresChoosing 10 sites as a set reaches 51% more exposed footpath and about 3 times the corridor gap of the plain top 10, for a 6-point lower average score.
Says how sure it is14 of the top 20 stay in the top 20 in at least 80% of 2,000 plausible weightings. The disagreement between independent canopy maps is shown on screen.
Protect before planting75 sites are already green by satellite and are flagged for an ecologist before any planting, in Sime Darby Property's Avoid, Regenerate and Conserve terms (a screening suggestion, not a finding).
From ranking to the groundA Tracker follows each site from shortlist to visit, decision and planting; blind field sheets and a validated import compare field results with the model. A real site list drops in with one command, tested to reproduce our scores exactly.
Built to be checkedOne command rebuilds every number from free data in about 4 minutes. Automated tests run on every push, and the published test counts are generated from the code.

What a site goes through, and what the tool will not let a planner claim

stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> Shortlisted: picked from the ranking
  Shortlisted --> VisitPlanned: a person will look
  VisitPlanned --> Visited: inspector, date, evidence
  Visited --> Approved: five checks OK + named, dated review
  Visited --> NotSuitable: named, dated, with a reason
  NotSuitable --> VisitPlanned: next-ranked replacement
  Approved --> Planted: a person marks it planted
  Planted --> [*]: survival from inspections only

The five field checks are land access, utilities, space and soil, maintenance access and existing trees. Each starts as Not checked; completing them is never an approval on its own.

5. HOW IT WORKS

A measured pipeline, served offline

The build turns imagery and map data into versioned files; it needs Earth Engine and the network. The runtime serves those committed files with neither, so the demo works offline and on a read-only host.

Steps 1 to 5 run at build time (Earth Engine and the network). Steps 6 and 7 run anywhere with neither, including the public deployment.

How a site is scored

Three scores from eight named inputs, then a weighted total. There are no labels to fit to, so nothing is trained; every number traces to its inputs. The planner can change the three right-hand weights with sliders.

Heat, greenness and built-up share are percentile ranks within the township ("hotter than 90% of sites"), because 100 m thermal data supports ranking, not exact temperatures. Distances use a smooth decay. The heat term is pulled towards the middle where ground temperature had to be filled in from neighbouring pixels.

6. EVIDENCE

What we checked, and what we did not

We checked consistency and robustness thoroughly. We have no ground truth, so none of this shows the ranking is right; it shows it is consistent and stable.

CheckResult
Imagery go or no-go, before building anythingGO for both periods and both satellites: only 27 of 144 Sentinel-2 scenes in the baseline were under 40% cloud, yet every pixel had at least 3 clear looks.
Canopy threshold sensitivityAbout 18%, 10% and 3% canopy at NDVI 0.5, 0.6 and 0.7, with a year-to-year swing of about 5 points. So canopy is used to rank sites, never quoted as a percentage.
Independent cross-checkESA WorldCover about 28%, Google Dynamic World about 7% falling to 4%, ours about 10%. Best agreement at NDVI 0.6 (F1 0.51).
Rank steadiness14 of the top 20 stay in the top 20 in at least 80% of 2,000 settings of the weights and judgement constants; rank correlation with the median ranking 0.999.
Method agreementA heat-only ranking shares none of our top 20 sites; an equal-weights version shares 18 of 20. The weights matter, which is why the planner sets them.
Before and afterOur canopy proxy moved from 9.7% to 10.4%, inside its yearly swing: reported as "no detectable change", not as growth.
ReproducibilityA full rebuild took about 4 minutes and reproduced the committed sites; every build carries a data version and stale scores are flagged.
Your own site listScoring a simulated site's own centre point through the import reproduces its committed scores and rank exactly (automated test).
Software qualityPython, model and frontend unit tests run in CI on every push; a real-browser test covers every page, keyboard use, hostile data and an accessibility scan.

What we did not do

  • No ground truth: no tree inventory, LiDAR, drone survey or field plots.
  • No real planting-site list: the 564 sites are simulated 30 m cells on open land, and every screen says so.
  • No measured benefit: reach numbers count what lies near the sites; they are not cooling, health, survival or carbon results.
  • No second township, no field walk, no user test yet. The field kit is tested on synthetic answers only, and the usability protocol is written but not yet run.
7. IMPACT

What the first sites reach, and how a pilot would measure benefit

Today Canopia measures reach (what lies near the sites, each footpath point, home and gap counted once), not benefit. For the first 50 sites in the default ranking:

5,400 mof footpath with little tree cover within 50 m (8% of the township's)
349 of 968mapped homes within 250 m
70%of the approximate corridor's gaps (about 1,150 of 1,650 m) within 50 m

Where Canopia sits, and how it repeats

A 2025 comparison of five tools in Austin found they agree on broad areas but differ on specific top sites, which is why Canopia shows how steady each pick is. We do not claim a new satellite method or to beat these tools.

Commercial case (hypotheses, to confirm with Sime Darby Property)

  • First customer: the City of Elmina landscape operations team, with the sustainability team as reporting partner, through a bounded pilot: real sites, a sampled field inspection, and a comparison with the current shortlisting process.
  • Value multiplies because the same pipeline and screens re-run for another township or question: more townships, corridor monitoring each season, where to inspect first after planting.
  • A real site list drops in today: python -m backend.my_sites scores any list of points inside the study area with the same measurements and model, offline.
  • Another township is estimated at a day or two of engineering (our estimate; not yet run elsewhere). What is Elmina-specific in the code is audited in the repository.
  • Costs are low on data (free imagery and maps); the main costs are people and field checks.
  • Licensing, stated openly: our Earth Engine registration is noncommercial. A runnable OpenStreetMap-only fallback exists; it is a proximity screen, not a second model, and whether the satellite workflow may be used commercially is a separate decision.

Limitations

LimitWhat it means
Simulated sites30 m cells on OpenStreetMap open land, not a real planting list; OpenStreetMap under-maps parts of the township.
Approximate corridorBuilt from OpenStreetMap parks: about 2 km of connecting spine against the stated 5 km. Connectivity results inherit this until the official polygon is supplied.
Coarse thermal dataLandsat surface temperature is about 100 m native; about 18% of the area is filled from neighbouring pixels for site scoring.
Canopy is a proxyNDVI at or above 0.6, not a tree count; strongly threshold-dependent and noisy year to year.
Siting screenFlags mapped roads, buildings and footpaths near a site; it is not a wildlife assessment.
Records are localTracker entries are saved in one browser; a shared, audited version is the first thing a real pilot would build.
