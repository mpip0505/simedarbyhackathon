# User flow

Written for: the pitch writers and judges asking "who uses this, and what happens next?". It separates what the app does today from the real-world steps that follow.

**One line for a slide:** Explore, Understand, Prioritise, Evaluate, Shortlist, Inspect, Plan the work, Plant and maintain. All eight are in the app as the planner's own records; the real-world work they record, and a shared audited version, are the pilot.

## The main user
A Sime Darby Property **landscape or sustainability planning officer** (hypothesis, to confirm with mentors), not the general public. Their question: *"We have a limited number of trees to plant this season. Where should we plant first, and why?"*

## The journey, in three stages
**Stage A: Plan (Plant first).** Explore the map and the five top sites, understand each one in a sentence, change what matters, evaluate what the first sites reach and how five ways of choosing compare, and add sites to a planting plan.
**Stage B: Verify (Pilot plan, steps 1 and 2).** For each shortlisted site, record five field-feasibility checks that start as Not checked, give it a status and an inspection batch, download a blind field sheet, and **import the completed sheet back**. The app compares each observation with the model's proxy (agree or differ), asks the planner to keep or reject the site, requires a reason for a rejection and offers next-ranked replacements.
**Stage C: Implement and learn (Pilot plan, steps 3 and 4).** Group approved sites into planting phases, enter your own capacity and costs, see the unique reach of the shortlist, download a work schedule, and, once a person marks a site Planted, record each tree with its species, owner and follow-up inspections. The survival report counts only inspected trees.

Assessment additions: site cards and step 1 show mapped conflict triggers separately from field checks; Plant first can filter to those triggers. Step 3 offers a resident exposure scenario from explicit assumptions and unique building reach, with an export. Pilot plan links to a separate OSM-only candidate map/list that needs no Earth Engine artifact to rebuild. These are screening/scenario tools, not field outcomes; see [assessment-response.md](../assessment-response.md).

A second view, **Before and after**, is for the sustainability analyst: two satellite periods side by side, greenness or ground heat, year by year, and how sure we are.

## What the plan is, and is not
Everything in the Pilot plan is the **planner's own record**, stored in the browser on that device. It is not a planting-management platform: no logins, no shared database, no audit trail, and nothing is inspected, approved or planted by the tool. A status such as Planted only records what someone typed; completing the five checks never approves a site; an unknown stays unknown; a tree with no inspection is never counted as surviving. Making it shared and auditable would be the first thing to build in a real pilot.

## How to say it to judges
"Our planner starts with a planning question. The map ranks candidate sites; each one explains why. They change what matters, see what the first sites reach, and shortlist sites. Then the Pilot plan takes over: five ground checks per site that start as Not checked, a blind field sheet to send out, and the completed sheet brought back to see where the field agrees or differs from the model. They keep or reject each site, find a replacement, group the approved ones into phases using their own capacity and costs, and later record the trees and their survival. It is the planner's own record, not an approval, and the real inspections are the pilot we would like to run with Sime Darby Property."
