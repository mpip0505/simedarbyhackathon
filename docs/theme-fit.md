# Theme fit: how the tool maps to the hackathon themes and to Sime Darby Property's stated priorities

Written for: the pitch writers (the Strategic Fit and Theme Relevance criterion, 15%). Developer targets and facts are **stated by Sime Darby Property in its own material**
(press releases, its website and sponsored partner pages) and are attributed as such; several tree counts in that material conflict, so none is used here
([pitch-fact-check.md](pitch-fact-check.md)). Teams may enter one track only; **we enter Planet**, and describe People as a co-benefit, not a second entry.

## The two themes (official wording)
- **Planet:** sustainable living and environmental regeneration; making sustainable practices easier for residents, communities and businesses.
- **People:** health and wellbeing, safety, and community connections.
Source: the [official hackathon page](https://www.simedarbyproperty.com/universityhackathon/) and launch coverage in The Star and EdgeProp. The brief also asks for technology, data or AI, solutions that can scale, and community-based approaches combined with technology.

## Why Planet is the honest track
The tool decides where trees go first, using satellite and map data, to support environmental regeneration inside a township. It does not measure or improve health, safety or
community connection, and we do not claim it does. People appears only as **who benefits where the sites are**: footpaths without shade near homes.

## Mapping to what the developer says it is doing
| Developer statement (attributed) | What the tool does about it | What it does not do |
|---|---|---|
| A **5 km Urban Biodiversity Corridor** linking six parks in City of Elmina, with more ecological corridors planned (developer material) | a corridor-connectivity score; the planting sites nearest corridor gaps rank higher; reach numbers show corridor gap length near the top sites | uses an **approximate** corridor from OpenStreetMap (about 2 km of spine); the official outline would replace it |
| A stated target of **50,000 endangered (IUCN-listed) trees by 2030**, and a nursery growing native and endangered species (developer material) | helps choose where the next plantings of any species could go first | no species or soil model: species choice needs the developer's landscape data |
| **Net zero by 2050**, with nature-based solutions named among the levers (developer press release, November 2023) | a documented, repeatable method for where to plant, which supports reporting | no carbon or sequestration figure, and none is claimed |
| Walkable township with a long walking and cycling track (developer material) | a shade score for unshaded footpath near each site; reach shows footpath with little tree cover near the top sites | no measure of walking, safety or health |
| Environmental education and research at the Elmina Rainforest Knowledge Centre (developer material) | open method and a plain-language "why this site" on every site | none |

## The People co-benefit (said carefully)
Where the shade score is high, a site is near footpath where the satellite sees little tree cover; where reach is high, a mapped home is within 250 m. In the first 50 sites that is about 5,400 m of footpath
with little tree cover (8% of the township's) and 349 of 968 mapped homes ([impact_reach.md](../ml/reports/impact_reach.md)). **That is reach, not benefit:** it counts what lies near the sites;
it does not show anyone is cooler, healthier or safer. Say "shade where people walk", not "healthier residents".

## Wording for the pitch
- Planet, one sentence: "We help Sime Darby Property decide where to plant first, so each planting moves its corridor, shade and heat goals forward."
- People, one sentence: "The same ranking points at footpaths with no shade near homes, so residents who walk are the ones who would notice."
- Do not say: "improves health", "reduces heat illness", "saves lives", "net zero", "carbon", "proven", "accurate".
